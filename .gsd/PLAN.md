## U1 User accounts and login API (roadmap item 2; complete, T-069 to T-084)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-05): password plus **server-side sessions**; **open self-registration**; **one graph per user**, its id derived from the user id and never taken from a client; a new component `src/users/` (logic over SQLite, no HTTP) and a thin **HTTP API** on `node:http`; no new dependency (`node:crypto` scrypt for passwords). Proposed, to confirm in T-069: the first account created becomes the admin; roles are only `user` and `admin`; the "details" of a user are username, display name, optional email (never verified or mailed: the Pi has no outbound mail) and timestamps; no password-reset email (an admin resets passwords); registration can be switched off by configuration. Open registration makes throttling and the account-enumeration rules part of the work, not an extra.

## C1 The CaCi controller: capture and browse over the user API (roadmap item 1; complete, T-085 to T-095)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-06): the CaCi controller is the **authenticated layer that a PWA calls**: signed-in routes to propose a filing for a note, approve or reject it, and browse **your own** graph (no editing or deleting items yet, no question answering: neither exists). **Free demo model by default; the real Anthropic model only when the operator switches it on** (`CACI_LLM=anthropic` plus `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), applying to every account, with the README saying plainly that then every account's notes go to Anthropic and nothing is anonymised (no per-account consent for now). **Pending proposals stay in memory**, owned by the account, with expiry (lost on restart). The uncommitted "Customer Adapter" in `architecture/container.mmd` stands for the user accounts side: renamed and committed in T-085. Proposed by me, to confirm in T-085: a new component `src/caci/` (the platform controller: it composes the user controller, the capture controller of `src/app/`, the LLM component and the graph store, and holds the rules that belong to no one of them: ownership of proposals, per-account limits, the graph always coming from the session), with the HTTP routes added to `src/api/` and the wiring in `src/service/`; the existing `src/app/` capture controller is unchanged and stays the engine; node and category ids appear in **query strings, not paths**, because ids are opaque (the model may mint a category id that no path pattern allows); an account may have at most 10 pending proposals (the capture controller's own limit is global) and 30 new proposals an hour (each costs a model call), both configurable; administrators get **no** access to other people's graphs through this layer.

## C2 Circles: groups of people with roles (roadmap item 3, part 1; complete, T-096 to T-108)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-07): a **circle is a shared group**, not tied to one person: a user may be a carer in one circle and be cared for in another, and each user keeps their own brain graph. **This milestone is membership and roles only**: create a circle, invite, accept or decline, assign and change roles, leave or remove. **No member can see or change anyone's graph through a circle yet**; the aim of circles is that members get *restricted* access to each other's brains, and **only CaCi and the LLM ever change a brain, as a result of actions performed (notes added etc.), never a member directly**; that sharing is a later milestone, after pseudonymisation and the legal groundwork (Backlog). **Invite, then the invitee accepts**; either side can leave or remove at any time. **A small fixed set of roles with environment-agnostic names**, so CaCi stays a generic platform that later requirements (care, family, teams, any other setting) can be targeted onto by labels and policies rather than by changing the code. Proposed by me, to confirm in T-096 (a new `specs/R-004-circles.md`): roles are `owner` (everything, including deleting the circle and making owners), `manager` (invites and removes people and moves them between `member` and `observer`; cannot touch owners or managers), `member` and `observer` (see the circle and its people, and leave; the difference between them is for the later sharing work, so it is recorded but carries no extra power yet); a circle always has at least one owner; the circle controller is a new component `src/circles/` in the existing `users.db` (a shared SQLite file, as the sessions are), taking a session token and resolving it through the user controller, with routes in `src/api/` and wiring in `src/service/`; an invitation names a username and a role, expires after 7 days, and the answer to an invitation is the same whether or not that account exists (so it cannot be used to probe for accounts); limits: 20 circles per person, 50 people per circle, 50 open invitations per circle (settable); when an account is deleted its memberships and invitations go with it, and a circle it solely owned passes to its longest-standing manager, else its longest-standing member, else is dissolved. ## S1 Shared circle brains: read-only, per-category grants (roadmap item 3, part 2; T-139 to T-152)

## K1 Controllable LLM communication: the controls as text, outside the code (R-012; T-195 to T-204; planned 2026-10-13; a feeder for the Test the Brain story)

## B1 Brain quality evaluation: does the brain beat replaying the text? (R-013; T-205 to T-214; planned 2026-10-14)
Owner decisions (2026-10-14): the purpose is **quality of fact retrieval, not performance**: information added early must not be forgotten when relevant, and **a later fact that overrides an earlier one must win** (the 2 x Tablet A then 1 x Tablet B case). The central question: **does the bipartite-graph brain answer better than simply keeping all the input as text and replaying it?** So: a **library of dated inputs that can be replayed** and a **comparison of the outcomes to questions**, run **against the real models**, to compare models too. Answers to my questions: the **brain arm uses `status` as it is** (every note filed through the real categoriser into a real graph, each question asked as a status request, so the real product path is measured and weak results point at what to build); the **baseline is the full transcript, in time order**, same model and same answer format; answers are **scored by rules** (facts that must appear, stale facts that must not; several repeats because real runs vary); **I draft the first library of invented scenarios and the owner reviews it** (about 8 to 10 scenarios of 10 to 40 dated notes with distractors, 3 to 6 questions each).
Proposed by me, to confirm in T-205: a new dev tool `dev/brain-eval/` (`npm run brain-eval`, never published; **invented text only**, since real runs send the notes to Anthropic); scenarios are plain JSON files with dated notes and questions asked at checkpoints (after note k), each question tagged by kind (`recall`, `override`, `retract`, `multi`, `distractor`) with `must` groups (alternative phrases) and `stale` phrases (a stale phrase that appears with a "superseded" cue such as "no longer", "changed from", "previously" is tolerated and counted separately); the brain arm runs the **real product stack in memory** (real user, capture and CaCi controllers, real categoriser and status, injected clock so notes get their scenario dates, limits raised so only the real context budget cuts anything); the replay arm calls the same `status` capability with **every raw note as an entry, oldest first, with the limits lifted**, so the only differences are what the entries are (filed items with a title and summary, versus the raw text) and how many fit; separate **filing model** (default `fast`) and **answering models** (default `fast`, `balanced`, `deep`), **3 repeats** of each answer; a **diagnostic per question** that says where a fact was lost (never stored by filing, stored but not in the context sent, in the context but not in the answer); a **filler option** (`--pad N`) that inserts deterministic unrelated notes to push the brain past its context budget (the test of "early facts forgotten"); a **demo mode** (free, deterministic) for the plumbing and an ideal-answerer and two bad answerers that prove the scoring discriminates; a **comparison command** for two saved runs (also the way to compare two controls folders, using `--controls DIR` from K1); the real run is the owner's, with a printed plan and `--yes`, as for the other evaluations. **Out of scope:** changing the brain (supersession, better retrieval, a dedicated answer capability, a hybrid arm, a recent-window arm); those are Backlog items to decide on the evidence. **One thing to expect and check: the brain stores only a title and a one-line summary of each note, never the raw text, so some detail may be lost before any question is asked; the per-question diagnostic exists to show this.**

## V1 The current-view brain: a new fact replaces the old one (R-014; T-215 to T-231; planned 2026-10-19)
Owner decisions (2026-10-19), from the first real brain-eval run (the brain keeps both the old and the new fact and leaves the model to choose): **the brain holds only the current view; when a note brings a fact that changes an existing one (a dosage), the old information is deleted**, so the graph stays clean, small and accurate over years; **history is not the graph's job** (old notes can be analysed elsewhere, later); the replacement is **automatic**, with **a log of what was added, removed and updated for the owner to review** (the ledger, as a review log); it applies to **personal and circle brains**; and the **brain-eval scoring fix comes first**, so the before and after are trustworthy.
Proposed by me, to confirm in T-216 (a new `specs/R-014-current-view-brain.md`): the model never writes delete operations. **The controller finds candidate existing items** (a deterministic, cheap word-overlap search of the scope's own items, newest first on ties, at most 8, within a character budget) and shows them to the categoriser in a delimited block; the categoriser may add one field, **`replaces`: a list of candidate item ids** (at most 5), and the guard accepts only ids that were shown, not duplicated, not the new item; **the controller, not the model, turns them into `deleteNode` operations** in the same atomic write as the new note, so the old fact goes in the same step the new one arrives; **only items are replaced**, never categories directly, and a category left with no items is tidied away after the write (so the graph stays clean; a grant that named it simply shows nothing); the preview lists what will be removed (transparency, not a second approval); in a circle, anyone who may capture there (owner, manager, member) may replace any item in it, as the item they replace is only ever one the model was shown from that circle; **the ledger** records, after a successful write, one entry per change: when, which scope, who, the action (`added`, `updated`, `removed`), the item and, for a removal, the removed title and summary (capped) so a wrong deletion can be recognised and re-entered by hand; the ledger is a review log, **not** history and **not** used to answer questions; it is kept for `CACI_LEDGER_DAYS` (default 90), read by anyone who may read that scope, and lives in `users.db` (schema version 6) as its own small store with its own conformance suite; the graph write and the ledger append are in two databases, so a failed append leaves the change in place and is reported to the operator (a known gap, recorded as a residual risk). **Honest risk, said up front:** an automatic deletion by a model can be wrong and lose a fact; the caps, the candidate-only rule, the same-scope rule and the ledger are the controls, and the brain-eval gains scenarios that add a different fact (which must not replace anything) and a `lost at replace` diagnostic so a wrong deletion is measured, not guessed. Out of scope: restoring from the ledger, history questions, replacing part of a note (an item is a whole note), per-item approval of removals, and deleting categories by the model.
