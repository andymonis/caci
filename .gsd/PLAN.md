## U1 User accounts and login API (roadmap item 2; complete, T-069 to T-084)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-05): password plus **server-side sessions**; **open self-registration**; **one graph per user**, its id derived from the user id and never taken from a client; a new component `src/users/` (logic over SQLite, no HTTP) and a thin **HTTP API** on `node:http`; no new dependency (`node:crypto` scrypt for passwords). Proposed, to confirm in T-069: the first account created becomes the admin; roles are only `user` and `admin`; the "details" of a user are username, display name, optional email (never verified or mailed: the Pi has no outbound mail) and timestamps; no password-reset email (an admin resets passwords); registration can be switched off by configuration. Open registration makes throttling and the account-enumeration rules part of the work, not an extra.

## C1 The CaCi controller: capture and browse over the user API (roadmap item 1; complete, T-085 to T-095)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-06): the CaCi controller is the **authenticated layer that a PWA calls**: signed-in routes to propose a filing for a note, approve or reject it, and browse **your own** graph (no editing or deleting items yet, no question answering: neither exists). **Free demo model by default; the real Anthropic model only when the operator switches it on** (`CACI_LLM=anthropic` plus `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), applying to every account, with the README saying plainly that then every account's notes go to Anthropic and nothing is anonymised (no per-account consent for now). **Pending proposals stay in memory**, owned by the account, with expiry (lost on restart). The uncommitted "Customer Adapter" in `architecture/container.mmd` stands for the user accounts side: renamed and committed in T-085. Proposed by me, to confirm in T-085: a new component `src/caci/` (the platform controller: it composes the user controller, the capture controller of `src/app/`, the LLM component and the graph store, and holds the rules that belong to no one of them: ownership of proposals, per-account limits, the graph always coming from the session), with the HTTP routes added to `src/api/` and the wiring in `src/service/`; the existing `src/app/` capture controller is unchanged and stays the engine; node and category ids appear in **query strings, not paths**, because ids are opaque (the model may mint a category id that no path pattern allows); an account may have at most 10 pending proposals (the capture controller's own limit is global) and 30 new proposals an hour (each costs a model call), both configurable; administrators get **no** access to other people's graphs through this layer.

## C2 Circles: groups of people with roles (roadmap item 3, part 1; complete, T-096 to T-108)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-07): a **circle is a shared group**, not tied to one person: a user may be a carer in one circle and be cared for in another, and each user keeps their own brain graph. **This milestone is membership and roles only**: create a circle, invite, accept or decline, assign and change roles, leave or remove. **No member can see or change anyone's graph through a circle yet**; the aim of circles is that members get *restricted* access to each other's brains, and **only CaCi and the LLM ever change a brain, as a result of actions performed (notes added etc.), never a member directly**; that sharing is a later milestone, after pseudonymisation and the legal groundwork (Backlog). **Invite, then the invitee accepts**; either side can leave or remove at any time. **A small fixed set of roles with environment-agnostic names**, so CaCi stays a generic platform that later requirements (care, family, teams, any other setting) can be targeted onto by labels and policies rather than by changing the code. Proposed by me, to confirm in T-096 (a new `specs/R-004-circles.md`): roles are `owner` (everything, including deleting the circle and making owners), `manager` (invites and removes people and moves them between `member` and `observer`; cannot touch owners or managers), `member` and `observer` (see the circle and its people, and leave; the difference between them is for the later sharing work, so it is recorded but carries no extra power yet); a circle always has at least one owner; the circle controller is a new component `src/circles/` in the existing `users.db` (a shared SQLite file, as the sessions are), taking a session token and resolving it through the user controller, with routes in `src/api/` and wiring in `src/service/`; an invitation names a username and a role, expires after 7 days, and the answer to an invitation is the same whether or not that account exists (so it cannot be used to probe for accounts); limits: 20 circles per person, 50 people per circle, 50 open invitations per circle (settable); when an account is deleted its memberships and invitations go with it, and a circle it solely owned passes to its longest-standing manager, else its longest-standing member, else is dissolved. ## W2 The web app with circles (roadmap item 6; T-117 to T-127)
Decisions (owner, 2026-10-08): the web app gains **everything a person needs to use circles**: create one, join (accept or decline an invitation), leave, view (the list, one circle, who is in it) and manage (rename and describe, invite, withdraw an invitation, change a role, remove someone, delete). **Screens get addresses (hash routes)** such as `#/circles` and `#/circles/<id>`, so the back button, a reload and a copied link work (this replaces the first slice's rule, R-005 D13, that screens are only states). **A person finds out about an invitation from a count on the home page, fetched when the page loads or when they press Refresh**: no background polling and nothing new on the server. Proposed by me, to confirm in T-117 (a new `specs/R-006-web-circles.md`, with a note in R-005): **no server change at all** (the fifteen circle and invitation routes exist; the policy stays as it is, the new files are listed and served like the rest); screens `#/` (home: your name, links to Circles and Invitations with the count, sign out), `#/circles` (your circles with your role in each, a create form, "show more" for more), `#/circles/<id>` (the circle, its roster, and what your role lets you do), `#/invitations` (open invitations with accept and decline); **ids taken from the address are checked against the exact shapes the service makes (`c` and 16 letters or digits) before they go anywhere near a request path**, and anything else is the home page; buttons are shown only for what your role allows, as a **hint only** (the service decides, and its refusal is shown as text; a test compares the page's role table with the service's own, over every combination); **destructive actions (delete a circle, remove someone, leave) ask again in the page** with a confirm and a cancel, never a browser dialog; each role is described in plain words beside the choice (owner: everything, including deleting and making owners; manager: invites and removes members and observers, never owners or managers; member: sees the circle and who is in it; observer: the same for now); the circle page says that **circles share no data yet** and that **everyone in a circle sees everyone's username and display name**; after inviting, the page says the invitation was recorded for that username and **never says whether such an account exists** (the service does not, and neither does the page); a circle that is not found is "no such circle, or you are not in it", the same words for both; on every change of screen the main heading takes the focus and the tab title follows. Order: T-117 spec, T-118 the circles API client, T-119 the router, T-120 the role hints, T-121 logic for the list, creating and invitations, T-122 logic for one circle and managing it, T-123 the pages for home, circles and invitations, T-124 the page for one circle, T-125 serve it, end to end and a hand check in Chrome, T-126 security review, T-127 README. **Each task that adds a file also updates the list in `src/service/web-app.ts`, the README's file table and the package check**, because those tests fail otherwise.

If the UI keeps growing, consider a small browser-level test (for example Playwright) as a dev-only dependency.
- **Measured (T-066, 2026-10-06, SQLite on a file, Node 22.19, this laptop):** reaching page 100 of the 5,000-item category costs about the same as its first page (p95 107 ms against 102 ms), so skipping to a cursor is **not** the problem; the cost is that a query from a category with depth 1 reads **the whole category's edges (and then its items) before it pages**, so the first page of a 5,000-item category takes about 100 ms whatever the page size. Ordinary categories are fine (random category, page of 50: p50 8.5 ms, p95 14.8 ms, under the 20 ms target). Decide on evidence whether it matters: a push-down or a streaming executor for single-seed depth-1 queries, or cursors that carry the adapter's own cursor so a page reads only what it needs. Not urgent for a household-sized graph.

- **Optimisation idea (deferred by the owner, 2026-10-04): a Jev classifier path.** TypeSafe's Jev is a "System One" model: one call to `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone` (bearer key) takes a `state` (the note) and typed questions (`choice` over up to 255 options with probabilities and confidence, `noul` yes/no probability, `score` 2 to 10 levels) and returns typed answers, no generated text; questions run in parallel; about $0.042 per million input tokens, 64k context, about 80 requests per second; not trained on customer data, zero retention enterprise-only; English best (docs: https://docs.typesafe.ai/). Fit: choosing among *existing* categories (a `choice` for the top pick or a `noul` per category for several, with calibrated confidence for the preview), and safer because the model can only return probabilities for ids we send, so a note cannot make it emit an operation. Does not fit: inventing new categories or writing an item title and summary, and it is not a `ModelClient` (it needs its own classifier port, with its own contract suite and a plain-HTTP client, so no new dependency). Proposed shape when revisited: Jev first to match existing categories (deterministic title and summary), escalate to the frontier `categorise()` when Jev says none fit or its confidence is low; compare Jev with the Anthropic tiers on a golden set in T-048 before wiring anything. Open points: quality on real notes is unmeasured (speed and cost figures are the vendor's), more than 255 categories need a pre-filter or one question per category, and it would be a second vendor receiving notes (extends the privacy question above). First step if picked up: a `jev:try` spike outside the gate, with the owner's key from the shell environment, never a file in the repo.
- **Privacy milestone (deferred by the owner, 2026-10-04): pseudonymise notes before they go to a model.** Needed before anyone's data other than the owner's is used, or before this stops being a personal proof of concept. Encryption cannot help at the model (it must read plaintext; homomorphic encryption is impractical for LLMs; confidential computing would need provider support, which I am not aware the Anthropic API offers; ask them), so the plan is pseudonymisation: a pure step in `src/app/` between the normaliser and `categorise`, so the LLM component is unchanged. Shape: `pseudonymise(note, categories)` returns tokenised text and context plus a mapping (`Dr Patel` to `PERSON_1`); `restore(proposal, mapping)` runs before the preview and rejects an unknown token or a leaked raw identifier; the mapping lives only as long as the pending proposal, is never sent, and any stored alias table is encrypted at rest. Decisions to make then: per-note tokens (more private, probably enough) versus stable aliases (the provider could link notes); detection by the owner's own contact list plus patterns (emails, phones, addresses, account numbers, dates of birth) first and an optional local detection model later; category names and data are part of what is sent and must be tokenised too; a preview of the exact outgoing text and a confirmation when something looks like PII but is unmapped (fail closed); the controller must also keep restored names out of anything sent on a later call. Test idea: the scripted client records every outgoing request, so a test can assert that no registered identifier appears in any of them; the lab can show the outgoing text. Limits to remember: it is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation (still personal data in law while re-identification is possible), indirect identifiers and health content remain sensitive, and a detector will miss things. Also decide then: a data processing agreement, retention and region terms with the provider, a local-model option for sensitive notes, and whether this is personal-use only (lighter obligations, but take legal advice) or a service for others (lawful basis, a risk assessment, a contract).
- Strengthen the evaluation before trusting the default tier: re-run with `--repeat 3` for variance; add harder and more ambiguous notes to the golden set (the first real run left fast and balanced both at 100%, so it cannot separate them); put some of the owner's own notes in a local `--golden` file outside the tracked files; and add a way to judge *quality*, not only allowed behaviour (for example the model's category choice compared with the owner's own labels, or a second model as judge). Also reconsider the `empty-graph` limit of 2 new categories, which penalised a reasonable 3-category answer from deep.
